# Analysis findings

The pinned snapshot shows growing combined assets with uneven growth across
the four banks, a loan book weighted toward housing, and different leaders
across lending, deposits and capital/liquidity measures. These are descriptive
results from the project's accepted data, not estimates of economic causation.

Monthly banking and macro observations end in **July 2026**. Capital and
liquidity observations end in **2026 Q1**. Publication dates differ from these
observation dates. This analysis does not claim to describe the latest release
available today.

## 1. Combined assets grew, with substantial differences between banks

In July 2026, the four banks held **AUD 4,209.35 bn** in resident assets on
the MADIS domestic, unconsolidated basis. This was **AUD 232.84 bn** above
July 2025, an increase of **5.86%**.

| Bank | Assets, AUD bn | Share of four-bank assets | Asset growth from July 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CBA | 1,252.45 | 29.75% | 8.68% |
| WBC | 1,192.15 | 28.32% | 9.68% |
| NAB | 983.84 | 23.37% | 0.11% |
| ANZ | 780.91 | 18.55% | 3.51% |
| Combined | 4,209.35 | 100.00% | 5.86% |

CBA had the largest balance, while WBC had the highest annual growth rate.
CBA and WBC together accounted for **58.08% of the selected four-bank assets**.
These shares use only the four banks as the denominator and are not market
shares of the entire Australian banking sector. Rounded bank shares may not
sum exactly to 100%.

The growth calculation measures reported balance changes. It does not separate
organic growth, acquisitions, reporting changes or other drivers, and it is
not an inflation-adjusted growth rate.

Source: MADIS Table 1, `Total residents assets`; calculation and chart rules
are in the [Overview guide](power_bi_overview.md).

## 2. Housing represented the largest selected loan component

July 2026 total loans and finance leases were **AUD 2,953.25 bn**. Housing
loans were **AUD 1,838.83 bn**, or **62.26%** of that total. Loans to
non-financial businesses were **AUD 892.45 bn**, or **30.22%**.

| Selected component | July 2026, AUD bn | Share of total loans and finance leases |
|---|---:|---:|
| Housing: owner-occupied plus investment | 1,838.83 | 62.26% |
| Non-financial business loans | 892.45 | 30.22% |

The two selected components cover **92.48%** of total loans. The remaining
**7.52%** covers other loan categories; this report does not decompose it.
Housing's larger balance identifies an area for further portfolio analysis.
The data here does not measure default risk, collateral quality or future losses.

Source: MADIS Table 1, owner-occupied housing, investment housing,
non-financial business loans and total resident loans/finance leases.
See the [Loans & Deposits definitions](power_bi_loans_deposits.md).

## 3. The largest bank depends on the selected business measure

CBA had the largest housing-loan and household-deposit balances in the four-bank
comparison. NAB had the largest non-financial business-loan balance.

| Measure | Largest bank | Balance, AUD bn | Share of that measure across the four banks |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Housing loans | CBA | 637.46 | 34.67% |
| Non-financial business loans | NAB | 276.52 | 30.98% |
| Household deposits | CBA | 467.73 | 36.76% |

Together the four banks had **AUD 1,272.39 bn** of household deposits, equal
to **49.85%** of their **AUD 2,552.49 bn** in total resident deposits.
Household deposits are one deposit category, not the full deposit base.
These balance rankings describe scale within the selected measures. They do
not establish profitability, efficiency or an overall ranking of bank quality.

Source: MADIS Table 1; bank values and accepted chart comparisons are in the
[Loans & Deposits guide](power_bi_loans_deposits.md).

## 4. Deposits grew faster than loans between June and July

| Measure | June 2026 | July 2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total loans, AUD bn | 2,940.45 | 2,953.25 | +0.44% |
| Total deposits, AUD bn | 2,531.61 | 2,552.49 | +0.82% |
| Aggregate loan-to-deposit ratio | 116.15% | 115.70% | -0.45 percentage points |

The deposit denominator grew faster than the loan numerator, explaining the
lower ratio over this one-month comparison. The July difference between the
two balances was **AUD 400.76 bn**. This is the arithmetic difference between
the selected resident loans and deposits, not a measure of an unfunded gap.
Other funding sources, maturities, asset liquidity and cash flows are outside
this calculation. The ratio alone does not demonstrate liquidity stress or
improved liquidity safety.

The aggregate ratio divides the sum of loans by the sum of deposits at one
month end. It is not the arithmetic mean of four bank ratios. Source and
measure: [Overview](power_bi_overview.md), MADIS Table 1.

## 5. Capital and liquidity ratios give different bank comparisons

At **2026 Q1**, combined reported CET1 capital was **AUD 226.60 bn** and
combined risk-weighted assets were **AUD 1,889.68 bn**. The reported ratios
are retained separately for each bank:

| Bank | CET1 ratio | LCR, mean | NSFR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ANZ | 12.40% | 131.80% | 114.90% |
| CBA | 11.60% | 132.80% | 115.50% |
| NAB | 11.60% | 131.60% | 115.70% |
| WBC | 12.40% | 132.20% | 112.30% |

ANZ and WBC shared the highest reported CET1 ratio. CBA had the highest
mean LCR, while NAB had the highest NSFR. WBC's NSFR was lowest among these
four observations, illustrating why a single ratio cannot supply an overall
bank ranking. The within-snapshot ranges were **0.80 percentage points** for
CET1, **1.20** for mean LCR and **3.40** for NSFR.

No bank-specific regulatory requirements, stress scenarios or compliance tests
are included, so this comparison does not assess regulatory headroom or solvency.
APRA ADI uses each entity's highest consolidation level, distinct from MADIS.
Do not combine these quarterly values with July domestic balances to construct
a cross-source capital or funding ratio.

CET1 history spans 2013 Q1-2026 Q1 and crosses the **2023 capital-framework
change**. It has not been restated onto a common framework. LCR and NSFR each
have 33 populated quarters per bank from 2018 Q1; earlier observations remain
missing. See [Capital & Liquidity](power_bi_capital_liquidity.md).

## 6. Macro conditions provide context, not an explanation of bank performance

In the July 2026 snapshot, the cash-rate target and interbank cash rate were
both **4.35%**, and the three-month bank-bill rate was **4.48%**. These are
monthly averages. The bank-bill rate was **13 basis points** above the target;
that difference is not an individual bank's funding cost or net interest margin.

Published All groups CPI annual inflation was **3.50%**, down from **3.80%**
in June, a decline of **0.30 percentage points** in the annual rate. This does
not mean the price level fell. The selected monthly CPI YoY series has only
16 observations, April 2025-July 2026. It does not cover the full banking history.

The project presents rates and CPI alongside bank balances. It does not
estimate correlations, time lags or causal effects. Claims that rate changes
caused lending growth or changed profitability would need further analysis
and additional data. Source series and available history are documented in
[Macro Context](power_bi_macro_context.md).

## Calculation and source notes

Amounts are aggregated from original AUD-million values before conversion to
AUD billion or rounding. Derived percentages use those unrounded values.
All shares above use the selected four banks and the named balance category.
Reported ADI ratios are read from the source, not recomputed from capital/RWA.

| Calculation | Inputs before display rounding | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Asset annual growth | July 2026 4209.3537; July 2025 3976.5102 AUD bn | `(current / prior - 1) * 100` |
| July loan-to-deposit ratio | Loans 2953.2491; deposits 2552.4870 AUD bn | `loans / deposits * 100` |
| June loan-to-deposit ratio | Loans 2940.4464; deposits 2531.6121 AUD bn | Same formula; subtract June from July for percentage-point change |
| Housing share | Housing 1838.8283; total loans 2953.2491 AUD bn | `housing / loans * 100` |
| Business share | Business 892.4528; total loans 2953.2491 AUD bn | `business / loans * 100` |
| Household-deposit share | Household 1272.3877; total deposits 2552.4870 AUD bn | `household / total * 100` |

Bank comparisons use the following unrounded asset inputs, in AUD bn:
